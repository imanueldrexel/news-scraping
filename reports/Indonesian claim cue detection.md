# Lexicons reach 95% only where cues close

**A lexicon + morphology gate can plausibly reach ~95% recall for the end-point gate, the plan markers and the core denial and hedge vocabulary. It cannot reach 95% on causal claims unless "causal claim" is defined as an *explicitly cued* one.** Closed-class cue detection reaches 90–95 F1 in every benchmark found: negation cues 92.3 F1, explicit discourse connectives 92–94, and the Indonesian temporal tagger IndoTimex 92.9 F1 on news. Open-class cues stall far lower: Wikipedia hedge "weasels" score 36.5 cue F1, and rule-based causal verbs reach 16% recall. About half of causal relations in English news carry no cue word at all, and no list can reach those. The alternatives do not beat the lexicon. The best trained sentence classifiers for causal news reach 88–91% recall at 81–84% precision. Trigger-word taggers reach 49–60 F1, and no Indonesian training data exists for any of the five cue types. NLI and LLM-judge checkers (SummaC, AlignScore, QAFactEval, FactCC, MiniCheck, GPT-4-class judges) answer a different question, "is this claim supported?". They do it at about 75–77% balanced accuracy in English, are weakest on causal links and dates, and have no validated Indonesian version. The design spec (SYS-196) picks the right architecture. But its measurement, as written, cannot tell a 95% gate from an 85% one. With 50 positives and a 70/30 split, a category's held-out set holds about 15 positives. Then a gate with true recall 0.95 fails the test 54% of the time, and a gate at 0.90 passes 21% of the time. The fixes are cheap:

- define the target over explicit cues;
- size the held-out set by its positives;
- enforce each gate on the side where a miss lets a false fact through;
- check the labels in both directions;
- add the affix classes the generator lacks;
- draw a fresh held-out set instead of reusing one.

## Closed cue classes reach 90–95%; open ones stall far below

The published benchmarks split along one line. Where the cue set is small and mostly single tokens, lexicons and trained systems both reach the low 90s. Where the cues are open-ended paraphrases, everything collapses, and the loss is almost entirely in recall. Negation cues on the *SEM 2012 Sherlock corpus reached **92.34 F1** (P 93.4 / R 91.3). The organisers explain why: "the set of negation cues is closed and comprises mostly single tokens" ([Morante & Blanco 2012](https://aclanthology.org/S12-1035.pdf)). Explicit discourse connectives in the Penn Discourse Treebank (Wall Street Journal news) show the cost of ambiguity. The connective string alone separates discourse from non-discourse uses at only 75.3 F; adding syntax lifts it to **92.3–94.2** ([Pitler & Nenkova 2009](https://aclanthology.org/P09-2004.pdf)). Hand-built temporal rules reach 90.7–91.9 extraction F1 in English. HeidelTime's automatically translated rules fall to **64.9–73.2**, with precision still 93–99 but recall at 49–59 ([Strötgen & Gertz 2015](https://aclanthology.org/D15-1063.pdf)). The one evaluated Indonesian resource for any of the five cue types is temporal. IndoTimex, a hand-built finite-state grammar, recognises temporal expressions in Indonesian news at **92.87 F1** ([Mirza 2016](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1604.08120)).

The open-class side looks very different. Hedge cues in biomedical text reached 81.3 cue-level F1 at CoNLL-2010. Wikipedia "weasel" hedges ("some say", "it is believed") reached only **36.5 F1, recall 25.7**, because they "have a diverse nature" ([Farkas et al. 2010](https://aclanthology.org/W10-3001.pdf)). NegEx, a pure trigger-phrase list for clinical negation, found **77.8%** of negated findings at 84.5% precision ([Chapman et al. 2001](https://www.dbmi.pitt.edu/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/A-simple-algorithm-for-identifying-negated-findings-and-diseases-in-discharge-summaries.pdf)). The pattern of high precision and incomplete recall matches what SYS-196 records about our own gates: each send-back added the forms QA tested, and the next held-out set failed again. The spec's own counts show where the gaps are. The missed *tertekan* appears in 1,320 articles, against 87 for the listed *dipacu*.

Causality is the hard case, and the reason is structural. In the PDTB only **46.75% of Contingency (causal plus conditional) relations are explicit**; the rest have no connective ([Pitler et al. 2008](https://aclanthology.org/C08-2022.pdf)). In the Causal News Corpus, 68% of causal relations contain a signal word, and 24–44% per split have none ([Tan et al. 2022](https://aclanthology.org/2022.case-1.28.pdf)). Even the explicit links are spread across word classes. In Causal-TimeBank, 180 of 318 explicit causal links are cued by connectives, **115 by causative verbs** and about 23 by context-dependent words such as "means that". A rule-based causal-verb module reached precision 0.82 but **recall 0.157** ([Mirza 2016](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1604.08120)). Indonesian news carries causation heavily in derived verbs (*dipicu, tertekan, terimbas, mendorong, berdampak*). The spec's frequency counts point the same way, although no Indonesian measurement of the split exists.

The last column matters for the design. For each gate, a miss on one side lets a false fact through, and a miss on the other side only costs a refusal. A missed causal word in the arc prose leaves a link unchecked. A missed causal word in the *source* leaves a true link unbacked, so the arc is refused and retried. Hedge and denial work the other way round: a hedge missed in the source lets "X is suspected of" back a flat "X did". The spec currently pools both sides and enforces both alike.

The table also forces a definitional choice the spec has not made. If DeepSeek labels "cause = yes" for a sentence like *"IHSG melemah di tengah aksi jual asing"*, which implies a cause without a cue word, no lexicon can fire on it. The measured recall then depends on how often the labeller marks implicit causes. The defensible claim is the one the evidence supports: **≥95% recall on explicit, lexically marked cues in a held-out sample of our own text**. The implicit share should be measured and reported as the accepted residual risk the spec already allows for.

## Morphology multiplies forms, collisions and gaps together

Generating affixed forms from roots is the right move. The evidence also says what it costs. IndoMorph (SEALP 2025) is the only Indonesian tool published as a word *generator*. It produces at least one correct form for **97.75% of 27,106 KBBI derived words**, but only **32.06% of its 83,597 generated forms are real words** ([Kamajaya & Moeljadi 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.sealp-1.7.pdf)). The analysers sit below that recall:

- **Sastrawi** stems 95.2% of affixed words correctly ([Mustikasari et al. 2021](https://eprints.umpo.ac.id/12871/)).
- **MorphInd's** finite-state analyser covered 84.6% of a corpus ([Larasati et al. 2011](https://www.springerprofessional.de/en/indonesian-morphology-tool-morphind-towards-an-indonesian-corpus/3708380)).
- **Aksara** lemmatises at 94.83% and tags POS at 88.2 F1 ([aksara](https://pypi.org/project/aksara/)).

Non-words cost nothing when the generated forms are only matched against text, because a non-word never appears. What costs precision is a generated form that is a **real word with another meaning**. *Tekan* yields *tekanan* ("pressure", a noun) and *penekanan* ("emphasis"). The spec's opt-in noun classes and `exempt.tsv` target exactly that.

The spec's class list leaves recall gaps that the literature and today's code both expose. IndoMorph's affix inventory includes the prefix *per-* and the circumfix *ke-…-an*. It also includes the enclitic *-nya* and four kinds of reduplication. The spec's `morph.py` has none of these:

- **The *per-* prefix.** Today's `_FUTURE_VERB` already matches *(di|mem)perkirakan*, and *diperpanjang* is a natural end-point verb. Neither can be expressed as a root row under the spec's classes.
- **The *ke-…-an* circumfix,** as in *kemungkinan*.
- **The *-nya* enclitic.** It produces some of the most common cue forms in news: *akibatnya, karenanya, rencananya, kabarnya, tampaknya*.
- **Reduplication,** as in *disebut-sebut* and *selambat-lambatnya*.

Standard-rule generation also misses forms that real writers use. IndoMorph's authors report that non-standard morphophonemic spellings such as *mempengaruhi* next to the standard *memengaruhi* are "frequent", and they suggest writing error rules to cover them ([IndoMorph](https://aclanthology.org/2025.sealp-1.7.pdf)). Loanwords and consonant clusters keep their first consonant (*memprotes, mentransfer, mengkritik*), which the spec's drop-p/t/k/s rules would get wrong.

The direction rule needs the same care. The spec's "auto" direction treats di-/ter- forms as backward and meN- forms as forward. It says nothing about *ber-* forms or nouns. *Berdampak (pada)* and *berimbas* run forward, while *berkat* runs backward. A noun like *pemicu* runs forward in "X menjadi pemicu Y" and backward in "pemicunya adalah X". Mirza's analysis of English causative verbs adds a further warning. Verbs like *make, allow, help* are causal only in a periphrastic construction, and "if their subjects are not events, they cannot be considered as causal markers" ([Mirza 2016](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1604.08120)). *Mendorong* ("drive" versus "urge") is the Indonesian case, which is why the spec's `purpose_exempt` flag matters.

The literature also points to a cheaper, more reliable way to find the forms. Run the generator in reverse over our own corpus. Stem every token in `articles.extracted_text` with Sastrawi, collect each surface form whose stem is a lexicon root, and rank those forms by frequency. That surfaces the forms writers actually use, non-standard ones included, and it would have found *tertekan* before QA did. Use it only to propose forms for review, because stemming over-merges. Sastrawi reduces both *berakhir* (an end-point verb) and *akhirnya* ("finally") to *akhir*. The spec's choice to match surface forms, not stems, at runtime is correct for the same reason.

## Trained heads see implicit claims but cannot name the word

A trained classifier is the only approach that can catch an implicit cause, but the evidence does not put it above the lexicon on recall. The best sentence-level systems on the English Causal News Corpus reach **86 F1, with recall 88.6–91.5 at precision 81–84**, trained on 2,925 sentences ([Tan et al. 2022](https://aclanthology.org/2022.case-1.28.pdf)). Their labels had a Krippendorff's alpha of only 0.35. Token-level *signal* (trigger-word) detection in the same task reached **49–60 F1**. Annotators agreed on signal spans at only 27–33%, so "which word is the cue" is poorly defined even for people. The other cue types follow the same order as for lexicons:

- **Negation cue taggers** reach 92.94 F1 (NegBERT) on a cue set of 30 types ([Khandelwal & Sawant](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.04211)).
- **Hedge sentence classifiers** on Wikipedia peak at 60–70 F1. Fine-tuned RoBERTa overfitted at 60.26 on about 10k sentences ([arXiv 2405.13319](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.13319v1)).
- **Indonesian has no data.** IndoLEM, IndoNLU, IndoNLI and NusaX contain no hedge, negation, causal, factuality or plan task ([Koto et al. 2020](https://aclanthology.org/2020.coling-main.66)).
- **No transfer study covers Indonesian.** Zero-shot negation-scope transfer works between European languages, up to 86.86 token F1 from Spanish to Russian, but none has been run from English to Indonesian ([Shaitarova & Rinaldi 2021](https://preview.aclanthology.org/setup/2021.naacl-srw.3)).

Two practical facts favour the spec's deferred classifier, which would be an e5 embedding head like SYS-37. First, few-shot heads are competitive. SetFit, a sentence encoder plus logistic regression, matched RoBERTa-large trained on 3k examples using **8 labelled examples per class** ([SetFit](https://huggingface.co/docs/setfit/main/conceptual_guides/setfit)). The gold set will hold more than that. Second, a sentence head is the wrong shape for a gate that must name its trigger. It says "this sentence states a cause" but not which words, so a refusal cannot tell the arc model what to drop. At about 80% precision, one in five of its refusals would be spurious. Its thresholds also need calibrating on arc prose itself: CNC's test sentences averaged 28 words against 17 in training, and that shift hurt the span systems.

The head's value is as a **union** with the lexicon, not a replacement. If the lexicon reaches 92% on explicit cues and the head flags half of what the lexicon misses, the pair reaches about 96%. It is also the only thing that sees implicit causes at all.

NLI and LLM-judge checkers test something else: whether a whole claim is supported by its source. Their accuracy is mediocre in English, and they are weakest on this project's error types. On LLM-AggreFact, the reference benchmark of 11 English datasets, the leaders reach **75–77% balanced accuracy** ([leaderboard](https://llm-aggrefact.github.io/)):

- Bespoke-MiniCheck-7B scores 77.4, and Claude-3.5 Sonnet 77.2.
- The 0.4–0.8B FactCG-DeBERTa and MiniCheck-Flan-T5 score 75.0–75.6.
- The older checkers trail ([MiniCheck](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.10774v2)): AlignScore 70.4, SummaC-ZS 67.9, QAFactEval 66.5 and SummaC-CV 62.1.
- On the older SummaC benchmark, AlignScore-large reaches 88.6 AUC and FactCC 68.8 ([AlignScore](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.16739)).

Three findings rule them out as the gate:

- **They are weakest on wrong links.** An invented "karena" between two true facts is what FRANK calls a Discourse Link error. QA-based metrics correlate *negatively* with it, and FactCC struggles with it ([FRANK](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2104.13346)). AggreFact later dropped the category as "uncommon" ([Tang et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12854)), so the headline 77% says nothing about invented causes.
- **Indonesian is weaker territory, worst on dates and numbers.** On IndoNLI's expert test set, an off-the-shelf XNLI XLM-R model reaches **73.8%** (75.7% translate-train) against 89.1% for humans. Temporal reasoning scores 68–70% and numerical reasoning **58–65%**, the categories an end-point or schedule check depends on ([IndoNLI](https://aclweb.org/anthology/2021.emnlp-main.821.pdf)). NLI-based hallucination metrics "fail for lower-resource languages" and miss single-fact hallucinations ([Kang et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10496)). MiniCheck's authors state that all their data is English.
- **LLM judges are not reproducible.** Verdicts change even at temperature 0: GPT-4 Turbo flipped 5.7% of votes in one study ([arXiv 2503.09347](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.09347)), and a 2026 study found "substantial inconsistency across all models" ([arXiv 2603.04417](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04417)). A gate built on one cannot be mutation-tested or replayed.

A dropped hedge (*diduga* turned into a flat claim) is formally an entailment strengthening. No benchmark measures it for summaries in any language. IndoNLI's crowd data also teaches a shortcut, "negation word means contradiction". Expect these models to key on surface words rather than meaning.

## Fifteen held-out positives cannot certify 95%

The spec's gold set follows best practice in its structure:

- the pool is drawn at random, not selected by the lexicon;
- the labeller writes out its trigger words;
- a manifest freezes the files by hash;
- QA owns the held-out split.

Its sizes and acceptance thresholds are too small for a 0.95 target. Recall is a proportion over *positives*, so its precision depends only on how many positives the held-out set holds. Under the spec's own "thin" rule, a category with 50 positives keeps about 15 of them in the 30% held-out split. To score ≥0.95 on 15 positives the gate must miss none. The figures below were computed for this report with exact binomial and Clopper–Pearson calculations:

- **A gate with true recall 0.95 fails that test 54% of the time.** One at 0.97 still fails 37% of the time.
- **A gate at 0.90 passes it 21% of the time,** and one at 0.85 passes it 9% of the time.
- **A perfect 15 of 15 shows only that recall is ≥0.82,** at the one-sided 95% bound.

At 200 held-out positives, a 0.90 gate passes under 1% of the time. Estimating recall near 0.95 to ±3 points needs about **215 positives per category** that gets its own number. *Certifying* "recall ≥ 0.95" as a lower bound needs about 400–1,000 positives and a gate that is truly at 0.97–0.98. With 100 positives, a gate truly at 0.97 fails that stricter certification 80% of the time.

Label quality matters as much as sample size, because label errors bias measured recall. Measured recall equals (1 − e)·R + e·f. Here R is the gate's true recall, e is the share of gold positives that are really negatives, and f is how often the gate fires on those. With R = 0.95, **5% false gold positives pulls measured recall to about 0.90**, and 10% pulls it to about 0.86. Missed implicit cues bias the other way: they drop hard positives from the gold set and push measured recall up toward the gate's own surface-cue behaviour.

The spec's spot-check is 50 sentences per category, half positive, with a 90% agreement pass mark. That tolerates 5 disagreements, which is consistent with a label error rate of up to 20%. Even a clean 25 of 25 positives bounds positive-label error only at 11%. Simple agreement on skewed classes also hides the error that matters. Cohen's kappa behaves badly when classes are imbalanced, and the literature recommends reporting positive and negative agreement separately ([Wongpakaran et al. 2013](https://bmcmedresmethodol.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/1471-2288-13-61)).

There are specific reasons to doubt LLM labels here:

- **LLMs beat crowds, not experts.** They outscored crowd workers by about 25 points on news and tweet classification ([Gilardi et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.15056)). But their best performance is "often too low to entirely replace human annotation" ([Ziems et al. 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.cl-1.8.pdf)).
- **On harder Indonesian tasks, humans win.** In the one Indonesian comparison, "human-generated annotations consistently outperformed LLM-generated ones in more intricate NLP tasks" ([Nasution & Onan 2024](https://doaj.org/article/dad423f5ac434c06bcb34a89583c6d5a)). Discourse cues are such a task.
- **LLM errors are systematic, not noise.** They shift with the model and the prompt wording ([Stolwijk et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.08404v1)).
- **The writer and the labeller come from one model family.** The arc prose is itself written by DeepSeek. A blind spot DeepSeek shares when writing and when labelling, such as an implicit cause it uses without noticing, hides in both the prose and the gold set.

Different model families fail on different items, which makes cross-family disagreement the efficient way to send items to human review. Routing human checks by model confidence has kept confidence intervals valid while cutting human labels by more than 25% ([Gligorić et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.15204)).

Reusing the held-out split is the last risk. Checking against the same holdout and fixing what it shows turns it into training data. The reusable-holdout work limits what the analyst sees to pass/fail, revealed only past a threshold ([Dwork et al. 2015](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1506.02629.pdf)). A fresh draw is the stronger check. New ImageNet and CIFAR test sets, built the same way as the originals, cut accuracy by 3–15 points, mostly through distribution shift ([Recht et al. 2019](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/recht19a)).

The spec puts `test_gate_coverage.py` in the unit suite, which dev runs constantly, so every run reveals held-out pass/fail. The held-out file also sits in `tests/fixtures`, readable by any dev session. Labelling is cheap enough that the stronger defence is affordable. At DeepSeek's list prices ($0.30 input and $1.20 output per million tokens), an output that lists only positive sentence IDs and their triggers keeps a 10,000-sentence draw near **$0.25**. A full five-category JSON record for every sentence would cost about $1.10, over the spec's $1 cap. Both figures are estimates from assumed token counts, and SumoPod's own prices are unverified.

## Ten changes that make the spec's 95% mean something

The architecture in SYS-196 ([#426](https://github.com/imanueldrexel/news-scraping/issues/426)) stands: lexicon data, generated forms, noun classes off unless listed, exempt rows found from the dev split, an equivalence-tested port, and a classifier only on evidence. The changes below are to what it measures and how.

At n = 30 a gate truly at 0.95 trips the two-month rule 20% of the time. A fresh draw is the only check that catches shift. |

Two things in the spec should stay as they are. NLI and LLM judges stay out of the gate, as §8 already implies: they are non-deterministic, unvalidated in Indonesian and weakest on links and dates. At most they belong in the shadow log the earlier causal-check report proposed. And the false-trigger budget against the ported baseline (§5) is the right counterweight. Each form added for recall is a possible refusal, and the budget is the only thing in the design that prices that cost.

## Conclusion

Closed-class versus open-class decides more than lexicon versus model. On closed cues, every approach converges near 92%. On open cues and implicit claims, every approach falls well short of 95%: the lexicon cannot see implicit claims, the classifier sees them but cannot name the word, and the judge checks support but is unreliable on links. So the realistic route to a defensible 95% is not a better detector. It is a narrower and more honest target: explicit cues, on the side of each gate where a miss lets a false fact through, measured on enough positives to tell 0.95 from 0.90. The implicit residue is then reported as a number instead of rediscovered by QA one synonym at a time.

The most useful thing the gold set will produce is a measurement no one has published: what share of causal, hedged, denied, end-point and plan claims in Indonesian news is explicit, and which roots and affixes carry them. That share sets the ceiling for any lexicon. If the implicit share of causal claims in arc prose turns out to be large, the case for the embedding head becomes a measured one, made before any month of missed recall triggers it.
